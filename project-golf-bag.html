Rideable Golf Bag — Northeastern University, Mechanical Engineering Design

Role Electronics & Controls
Tools Arduino, hand tools
Deliverable Functional prototype
Team 3 students

Overview

For a final Mechanical Engineering Design class project, I was assigned a group that was tasked with finding a problem and designing a solution. The agreed-upon project was a rideable golf bag to help physically limited people traverse a golf course.

Design & engineering

After trial and error sketching models and theorizing the design's behavior, the time came to produce a prototype. I, along with two members of our group, went to a hardware store to gather wooden supports, tape, cardboard, wheels, and screws to build our prototype. The two others constructed the frame while I assembled an Arduino, motor, and potentiometer circuit that functioned to power the motor by responding to the amount of current the potentiometer allowed to flow through it. The amount of current the potentiometer allowed to flow would enable it to mitigate the tire's RPM from the steering handlebar at the front of the design.

In spite of some initial struggles with the direction of the tire's revolutions, and the allowance for the spacing for components along the design, our design group finished well on time. The professor was greatly impressed that we had managed a functional and moving prototype, along with a cohesive presentation for it. Our group received overall good marks, and our audience was left with few questions for us to answer in the aftermath.
